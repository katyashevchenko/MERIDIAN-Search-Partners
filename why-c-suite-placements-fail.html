Why 65% of C-Suite Placements Fail in the First Two Years

Most failed executive hires are not skills failures — they are context failures. Here is what boards consistently underestimate.

→ Read the full article
M
Executive summary

What the data shows.

Roughly two-thirds of failed executive hires fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the candidate's competence. When a senior hire doesn't work out, the instinct is to conclude the search missed something about the candidate — but the more common root cause sits earlier: the mandate itself was never fully agreed before the search began.

A board that hasn't aligned on what success looks like in month six, twelve, and twenty-four will struggle to evaluate — or support — the person they hire, regardless of how strong that person is. Cultural misalignment tends to surface around month four to six, not in the interview process, because it takes that long for the operating friction to become undeniable.

The firms with the strongest retention numbers share three habits: they write the scorecard before opening the search, they test for cultural fit as rigorously as competence, and they treat the first 100 days as part of the placement — not a separate HR process handed off once the candidate starts.

Key findings

Four things boards should know.

Patterns consistent enough to be predictable — and preventable.

03

04

Skills gap
